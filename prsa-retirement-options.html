Step one — the split

A quarter can come out as cash. Three quarters stays working.

Under current PRSA rules you may take up to 25% of the fund as a retirement lump sum. The remaining 75% must be used to provide retirement income.

Step two — the lump sum

Your lump sum sits just €7,714.75 above the tax-free ceiling.

The first €200,000 of retirement lump sums taken in a lifetime is free of tax. Anything between €200,000 and €500,000 is taxed at 20%. Your 25% entitlement is €207,714.75 — so only the sliver above the line attracts tax.

Step three — the balance

Three routes for the remaining €623,144.25.

These are not mutually exclusive — the balance can be split between an ARF and an annuity, and the decision does not have to be made all at once.

Before you decide

Five things worth talking through.

  • 01You do not have to draw benefits now. A PRSA can generally be left until age 75. Deferring keeps the fund invested, but it also keeps it exposed to markets.
  • 02The €200,000 limit is a lifetime one. If any earlier scheme has already paid you a retirement lump sum, more of this one becomes taxable — we should confirm your full pension history before anything is drawn.
  • 03ARF and annuity is not either/or. Securing part of the balance as guaranteed income and leaving the rest invested is often the more comfortable answer than choosing one route for the whole €623,144.25.
  • 04The 4% minimum drawdown applies whether you need the money or not. From the year you turn 61 an ARF is treated as paying out at least 4% a year and taxed accordingly — currently €24,925.77 on this balance, rising to 5% from age 71.
  • 05Route C is expensive. Taking the whole balance as cash would put nearly all of it at the top marginal rate in a single tax year, and it ends the pension entirely.

Important information

The figures shown are for illustrative purposes only and are based on Irish pension and taxation rules as understood at the date of preparation. Pension, taxation and Revenue rules may change and may affect both the options available and the tax treatment applying when benefits are actually taken. All limits, rates and thresholds should be confirmed with your provider and with Revenue before you act.

The value of investments can fall as well as rise and you may get back less than the amount invested. Past performance is not a reliable guide to future performance. Income from an Approved Retirement Fund is not guaranteed; if withdrawals exceed investment returns the fund will reduce and may be exhausted during your lifetime. Annuity rates shown are illustrative only, are not a quotation, and vary by insurer, by the options selected and from day to day.

Lump sum figures assume no previous retirement lump sum has used any part of the €200,000 lifetime tax-free limit. The indicative net figure shown for taking the balance as taxable cash assumes a combined marginal rate of approximately 52% (income tax, USC and PRSI) and takes no account of your personal credits, rate bands or other income in the year.

Any retirement decision should be based on your own circumstances, objectives, risk profile and the rules applying at the time benefits are taken. This document is an illustration and does not constitute financial advice or a recommendation.
